\section{Scientific context}
% what was the question
Within the earth's climate, the poles are known to be extreme, yet very sensitive regions.
The Arctic, with its central ocean, partly covered in sea ice and circled by lands of permafrost, is particularly exposed to the changes caused by global warming.
Furthermore, it is located in the northern hemisphere sharing it with two thirds of the emerged lands, where nearly \perc{90} of the global population live. This exposes the Arctic to human activities, making it a core topic of geopolitical discords.
With a warming rate four times faster than the global average \citep{rantanen_arctic_2022}, the Arctic climate is both a complex and a very interesting system to study. 
Its particularities of solar exposure, atmospheric stability, temperature and remoteness (presented in \cref{sec:arctic_glimpse}), make it especially difficult to represent in a numerical model.

% l'objectif
The research project presented in this thesis, aimed to fill some of the knowledge gaps that strew our understanding of aerosols capable of forming cloud ice, as well as what we know of their impacts on Arctic clouds.
% the clouds
The latter, which are mainly stratiform clouds of low altitude, have in average a net warming effect of the surface, conversely to mid- and low-latitude clouds (c.f. \cref{fig:matus_CRE}).
They are in majority composed of ice crystal or a mix of ice and liquid droplets (c.f. \cref{fig:matus_cloud_frac}), which makes them especially sensitive to ice nucleating particles (INPs). Compared to cloud condensation nuclei (CCN)\----which are sufficiently abundant to rarely be a limitant factor to cloud formation \citep{storelvmo_aerosol_2017}\----INPs are scarcer by orders of magnitude, which makes them determinant for the cloud phase partitioning.
% the importance of INPs
A plume of INP can initiate the formation of ice crystals in a cloud, and helped by the Wegener-Bergeron-Findeisen (WBF) effect, causes a radical change in the cloud phase. The associated change in the cloud radiative effect (CRE) would thus affect the whole atmospheric radiative budget \citep{storelvmo_aerosol_2008}.
The misrepresentation of such effects in atmospheric models is one of the reason for the poor performance of Arctic climate modelling \citep{bony_clouds_2015}. 
% the knowledge on sources
The lack of knowledge on Arctic INPs\----what are the species involved and what are their emission sources\----, is one impediment to a better representation of INPs in models \citep{murray_opinion_2021}.
% the knowledge on ice nucleation
Although the science of ice nucleation has been studied since the 18th century, accurately representing the formation of ice crystals in climate models remains a big challenge. The parametrisations of heterogeneous ice nucleation abound \citep[e.g.][]{fletcher_active_1969,cooper_ice_1986, demott_predicting_2010, li_predicting_2022}, but they are often very specific to a region or to a particle species, which makes difficult the generalisation in a climate model.
Consequently, the models fail at keeping their role of efficient tools for the understanding of the atmospheric mechanisms.

In this modelling thesis, I used the Weather Research and Forecasting model (WRF), its version that includes chemistry (WRF-Chem), and the FLEXible PARTicle dispersion model (FLEXPART) to explore portions of these questions. %and eventually them.
My thesis research project consisted in three main steps: (1) getting a better understanding of the sources of Arctic INPs, (2) improving the modelling of particles that are potential INPs and investigating how to accurately convert them into INPs, (3) linking modelled INPs to the microphysics in order to investigate their effect on clouds properties.


% what about the INP sources
\section{Sources of Arctic INPs}
% the detection method
The first project of this thesis was the assessment of the potential source contribution function (PSCF) performances for the retrieval of short-lived atmospheric species.
The PSCF, or ratio method, is an analysis technique of Lagrangian model outputs that enables the identification of the likely emission sources of observed atmospheric species (\cref{P1sec:ratio_method}).
It is more advanced than single back-trajectory analyses\----which are extensively used in the atmospheric sciences community\----, yet, the PSCF assessment of its performances retrieving known sources of modelled tracers revealed that its outputs can be spurious.
Indeed, in order to evaluate the method, we set up modelled emissions of idealised atmospheric tracers in WRF-Chem and interpolated their concentrations as time series all around the Arctic to mimic in situ measurements. Then we used FLEXPART-WRF to compute Lagrangian back-trajectories, and finally applied the PSCF (\cref{P1sec:experiment}).
The emission sources were already known since they were modelled, which enabled the evaluation of the source identification method (\cref{P1sec:evaluation_metric}).
One of the main advantages of the PSCF is that it allows for the quantification of the sources respective contributions to the measured concentrations. However, the results of the assessment showed that the conclusions of its quantification can be wrong and misleading (\cref{P1sec:standard_method}).

Strong of this finding, and of the assessment setup that led to it, we conducted sensitivity tests in order to get a reliable source identification method.
We ended up with the \textit{improved ratio method}, that comprises the standard PSCF protocol with specific parameters and additional treatments (\cref{P1sec:improved_method}).
\vspace{\baselineskip}

% the analyses of INP
In possession of a method with known performances, we were ready to undertake the identification of Arctic INPs.
We used INP concentration data from three recent campaigns (MOCCHA 2018, MOSAIC 2020 and ARTofMELT 2023, \cref{sec:INP_sources}).
The analyses revealed that INPs originate from inside the Arctic region, with possible transport from inland Asia. Two main regions were highlighted: coasts of the Barents and Laptev seas, and coasts of Greenland.
These results suggest a predominance of dust particles among the observed INPs, but the study of MOSAIC warm INPs (active at \tempc{-10} and \tempc{-15}), showed possible oceanic sources that suggest the presence of marine organic particles in populations of warm INPs.
The study of primary biological aerosol particles (PBAPs) origins supported this observation, showing possible oceanic sources from ice free areas of the Arctic Ocean.

\section{Ice nucleation modelling and INP emissions}
In \cref{chap4}, we used the knowledge on the INP sources to improve the representation of aerosols particles capable of nucleating ice in WRF-Chem. 
Emissions of high latitude of dust (\cref{sec:dust_emiss}), marine particles organic aerosols (MPOA) from sea sprays (\cref{sec:seaspray_emiss}), and continental PBAP from fungal spore and bacteria (\cref{sec:pbap_emiss}) were added to the model.
The objective was to use the new modelled particles to reproduce in situ INP concentrations in central Arctic.
The challenge have been addressed with two approaches.

The first approach relied on the conversion of aerosol particles, after their transport in the atmosphere, by ice nucleation parametrisations.
Eight different parametrisations were used; among them, three were specific to dust particles, one to sea sprays, two to bacteria, one to fungal spores, and one was general and used all types of coarse-mode particles (\cref{sec:inp_param}).
In addition to the modelled particles, have also been used observations from the MOCCHA 2018 campaign to feed the parametrisations.
The results showed that it is difficult to reproduce INP variability with modelled particles as input of the parametrisations (\cref{sec:aerosols_to_INP}). Nevertheless, concentration levels were correctly reproduced with the parametrisation that uses the whole coarse-mode \citep{demott_predicting_2010}. %Furthermore, the levels of PBAP particles (bacteria and fungal spores), were
When the actual observed concentrations of particles were used to feed the ice nucleation parametrisations, the time variability was much better reproduced.
The dust parametrisations overestimated the INP concentrations. This is partly due to the hypothesis of a coarse-mode of \perc{100} dust particles. Therefore, the resultant overestimated INP concentrations suggested that the coarse-mode of the MOCCHA campaign was not full dust (\cref{}).
Concerning the biological particles observed during the campaign, the fluorescent PBAPs (fPBAPs), no parametrisation was able to predict high enough INP level to match the observed INP concentrations.
Indeed, the nucleation rate necessary for INPs to be driven by PBAPs is close to \perc{100} at \tempc{-20}.
From the current range of our knowledge on ice nucleation with PBAPs, the latter can not explain alone the INP concentrations during the campaign.
The fact fPBAPs are well correlated with the INP measurements suggests two things: (1) fPBAPs are transported with other types of particles capable of nucleating ice, (2) the fPBAPs observations underestimate the actual fPBAPs concentrations (\cref{sec:aerosols_to_INP}).

The second approach consisted in the emission of INP tracers from emissions of aerosols particles considered as potential INPs.
With ice nucleation parametrisations suited to the particle species, the latter was converted into INPs in five temperature bins, from \tempc{-5} to \tempc{-25}. 
The INP tracers where then transported in the modelled atmosphere, subject to wet and dry deposition, as well as in-cloud removal by activation (\cref{sec:method_inp_emission}).
This last process was the main thing that was missing in the first approach in order to faithfully reproduce the INP nucleation history.
The INP tracers were used to get insight on the spatial distribution of warm to colder INPs. Namely, it enabled the identification of zones where homogeneous nucleation can happen; opening the gate toward new implementation of INP-cloud interactions.
Interpolating the INP tracers concentrations at the location of the MOCCHA and MOSAIC campaigns made possible their comparison with INP observations (\cref{sec:second_approach}).
The variability was better reproduced than what was obtained with the first approach.
The general levels were consistent with the observed INP concentrations and better than those of the first approach.
These results gave confidence in the INP tracers modelling setup, which made us use it further in the following chapter in an attempt to link INPs to the cloud microphysics.

\section{Perspective of future investigations}
Whereas the work presented it this thesis has brought answers and insights to several research questions on INP-cloud interactions in the Arctic, it has also paved the way for many future investigations.
The perspectives brought by my PhD work can be classified in three categories: to be continued, going further, and openings on general questions.
\vspace{\baselineskip}

% apply the detection method to more INP data.
    % beginning with the whole MOSAIC data set
    % the PINE network
    % the tara polar station for 20 years of data
The improved ratio method for the identification of particle emission sources presented in \cref{chap3} calls to be used with more INP datasets.
I my analysis of INP sources, I did not make use of the entire MOSAIC 2020 campaign, focusing only on the cold season. Understanding the sources of the INP in spring and summer would be important to unveil the nature of warm season INPs. 
It would complete the results of the MOCCHA 2018 campaign analysis, and the comparison with the winter sources would give important insights on the seasonality of Arctic INP sources.

Beyond data from ice breakers campaigns, the development of more systematic long duration INP measurements with consistent instrumentation sets the conditions for better sources detection.
For example, the Portable Ice Nucleation Experiment (PINE) worldwide developing network is very promising \citep{mohler_portable_2021}. PINE is an automated and portable instrument that measures INP concentrations. Since a few years, it has been installed in many research stations around the world, including high latitudes.
The use of long duration measurements would enable the collection of unprecedented information on the sources seasonal variability. And the application of the ratio method on a network of simultaneous measurements performed with the same instrument would be a world first in the study of INP sources.

% tara
On longer time scales, the Tara Polar Station project\footnote{More information about the project on the Tara foundation website: \url{https://fondationtaraocean.org/goelette/tara-polar-station/}.} was launch this years with the objective of ten missions of eighteen months each.
It will provide INP concentrations measured across the Arctic Ocean over twenty years, giving insights on their spatial and temporal evolutions within the scales of global warming. These expeditions will enable hitherto unseen data for the detection of INP sources.

% pousser sur l'origine des nuages
The two side studies presented in \cref{sec:other_applications} showed the potential of the ratio method in being adapted and applied in various conditions.  
The study on the origins of cloudy air masses encourages further investigations by raising questions: is it relevant to categorise clouds by their origins? Is cloud phase dependant on air mass origin? Are mixed-phase clouds originating from particular transport patterns?
