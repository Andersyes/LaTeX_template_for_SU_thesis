% what was the question
Within the earth's climate, the poles are known to be extreme, yet very sensitive regions.
The Arctic, with its central ocean partly covered in sea ice and circled by lands of permafrost, is particularly exposed to the changes caused by global warming.
Furthermore, it is located in the northern hemisphere sharing it with two thirds of the emerged lands, where nearly \perc{90} of the global population live. This exposes it to the intensity of human activities, and makes it a core topic of geopolitical discords.
With a warming rate four times faster than the global average \citep{rantanen_arctic_2022}, the Arctic climate is both a complex and a very interesting system to study. 

Its particularities of solar exposure, atmospheric stability, temperature and remoteness (presented in \cref{sec:arctic_glimpse}), make it especially difficult to represent in a numerical model.

% l'objectif
The research project presented in this thesis, aimed to fill some of the knowledge gaps that strew our understanding of aerosols capable of forming cloud ice and of their impacts on Arctic clouds.
% the clouds
The latter, which are mainly stratiform clouds of low altitude, have in average a net warming effect of the surface, conversely to mid- and low-latitude clouds (c.f. \cref{fig:matus_CRE}).
They are in majority composed of ice crystal or a mix of ice and liquid droplets (c.f. \cref{fig:matus_cloud_frac}), which makes them especially sensitive to ice nucleating particles (INPs). Compared to cloud condensation nuclei (CCN)\----which are sufficiently abundant to rarely be a limitant factor to cloud formation \citep{storelvmo_aerosol_2017}\----INPsv are scarcer by orders of magnitude, which makes them determinant for the cloud phase partitioning.
% the importance of INPs
A plume of INP can initiate the formation of ice crystals in a cloud, and helped by the Wegener-Bergeron-Findeisen (WBF) effect, causes a radical change the cloud phase. The change in the cloud radiative effect (CRE) would affect the whole atmospheric radiative budget \citep{storelvmo_aerosol_2008}.
The misrepresentation of such effects in atmospheric models is one of the reason for the poor performance of Arctic climate modelling. 
% the knowledge on sources
The lack of knowledge on Arctic INPs\----what are the species involved and what are their emission sources\----, is one impediment to a better representation of INPs in models.
% the knowledge on ice nucleation
Although the science of ice nucleation has been studied since the 18th century, accurately representing the formation of ice crystals in climate models remains a big challenge. The parametrisations of heterogeneous ice nucleation abound, but they are often very specific to a region or to a particle species, which makes it difficult to generalise in a climate model.
Consequently, the models fail at keeping their role of powerful tools for the understanding of the atmospheric mechanisms.
