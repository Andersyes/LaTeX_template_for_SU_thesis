This thesis accounts for what has occupied me over the past three years. It is therefore not exclusively a record of my research work, but also, to a lesser extent, of what I have taught to students, shared with the public, as well as what I have learned, and everything that has nourished my thoughts and conversations with those around me.
The first chapter, and particularly its opening, thus takes the form of a broad conduit that gradually narrows toward the core of my research subject.
This is the first proposition of this thesis: keeping a constantly wide perspective on the state of the climate is necessary when studying any of its components, however specific the topic may be. The reader should therefore not be surprised to encounter discussions about clouds and microphysics only after some twenty pages.
\vspace{0.3cm}

The thesis is written in English, which is not my mother tongue. Some of my ideas may therefore appear slightly unclear, or awkwardly expressed, despite my best efforts to smooth out these variations in tone and rhythm. 
Nevertheless, I hope that the reader will sense, throughout their journey in this work, something of the pleasure I had in writing it.
