\section*{International and national science projects}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Climate Relevant interactions and feedbacks: the key role of sea ice and Snow in the polar and global climate system (CRiceS).

            The CRiceS project focuses on improving model predictions of the role of polar processes in the climate system that consists of the oceans, ice and snow cover, and the atmosphere. It is crucial to understand the role of the polar processes, such as feedback loops, in polar and global climate. One of the main ways scientists can improve our understanding of environmental change is to combine knowledge from different disciplines in a coordinated way.

            My work contributed to the work package 2 (Improved ocean-ice/snow-atmosphere processes in models) and to the core theme 2 (Aerosols and Clouds).

        \item Cloud-aERosol inTeractions and their impActs IN The earth sYstem (CERTAINTY)

            CERTAINTY aims to deliver the knowledge and models that provide improved confidence and representation of the role of cloud–aerosol-radiation interactions in climate and weather. This translates to better understanding and predictions of extreme events and facilitates planning climate mitigation and adaptation strategies for the good of the society.

            My work contributed to the work package 3 (Improved parameterization of cloud-aerosol interactions).

        \item Microphysical Process Characterization of Mixed Phase Clouds in the European Arctic (MPC)²

    The (MPC)² project aims to identify and better understand the interactions between the various physical processes involved in the life cycle of Arctic mixed-phase clouds. The objective is to improve the representation of these clouds in regional models in order to more accurately estimate their radiative impact within the atmospheric boundary layer. The approach relies on the implementation and analysis of data collected during four airborne measurement campaigns in the Svalbard region, combined with satellite observations, as well as on the use of models at different scales to develop and test new cloud parametrisations under contrasting environmental conditions.

            My work on the methodological approach of emission source identification (see Chap.~\ref{chap3}) contributed to the project. I used this method to identify the origins of the air masses which formed the clouds observed during the airborne campaign studied during the project (see Sect.~\ref{sec:cloud_origins}).

        \item Atmospheric Rivers and the Onset of Arctic Melt (ARTofMELT)

        ARTofMELT is an international interdisciplinary research expedition that aims to study the effects of episodic transport of hot and humid air from mid-latitudes to the Arctic.

            I worked in collaboration with Colorado State University for the production of a backward modelling dataset that covers the campaign. I ran the FLEXPART simulations, published the dataset on the Bolin Data Centre, and developed the analysis tools \citep{da_silva_backward_2025, da_silva_plotting_2025}.

    \end{itemize}
