\enabstract{
\hspace*{0.5cm} 
    Ice nucleating particles (INPs) are aerosols capable of helping the formation of ice crystals in clouds.
In the Arctic\----although global warming is four times faster there than the global average\----, temperatures are most of the time below the congelation point, and clouds composed of both liquid and solid phases are common. 
When modelling the Arctic climate, errors on the cloud phase partition have strong consequences on the model accuracy. This is due to the impact of cloud phase on the radiative budget, precipitations and cloud lifetime. 
All these properties are affected by aerosol-cloud interactions, and their representation in models suffers of the poor\----although growing\---- knowledge on INPs.

% what I did in this thesis
In this thesis, I address the role of INPs in Arctic aerosol-cloud interactions and their impacts on cloud properties in three steps. 
First, I develop, assess and apply a methodology for the identification of INP emission sources that rely on the Lagrangian model FLEXPART-WRF and observational data. Then I use this method in order to shed new light on the origins and the species implicated in recently measured INP concentrations.
Second, I implement new high-latitude aerosols sources in the regional model WRF-Chem, and use them along with ice nucleation paramtrisations to represent the dynamic of INP populations in the Arctic.
Finally, after the modelled INPs have been compared to observations, I couple them to the microphysics in order to investigate their effect on the Arctic cloud properties.

Results showed that, in central Arctic, INPs mainly originate from the Russian coasts of the Barents and Laptev seas, and the coasts of Greenland, where dust particles are known to be emitted.
However, INPs active at temperature above \tempc{-15} may originate from biological marine sources.
The implementation of these high-latitude emissions, and the use of corresponding ice nucleation parametrisations allowed for a realistic representations of Arctic INPs in WRF-Chem.
